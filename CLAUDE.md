# CL Security Survey

## Project objective
Develop a scientific survey on security attacks and defenses in Continual Learning (CL).

## Project structure
- `literature/`: original scientific papers and literature summary tables.
- `taxonomy/`: proposed taxonomies and classification schemes.
- `extra/`: supplementary materials that may be useful later.
- `CL_Security_Survey/`: local copy of the Overleaf project.
- `docs/`: project documentation, organized into:
  - `analysis/`: literature analysis, project inventory, and taxonomy comparisons.
  - `planning/`: survey structure, research plans, and project decisions.
  - `writing/`: drafts and writing-related materials.
  - `verification/`: citation checks, evidence verification, and consistency checks.

## Research principles
- Treat original scientific papers as the primary sources of evidence.
- Use summary tables as indexes to the literature, not as substitutes for the original papers.
- Never invent references, results, technical details, or claims.
- Distinguish verified facts from interpretations, hypotheses, and unresolved questions.
- When making claims about a paper, identify the supporting source whenever possible.
- Flag conflicting evidence, ambiguous classifications, and missing information instead of resolving them through unsupported assumptions.

## Workflow
- Before writing or modifying the manuscript, analyze the relevant materials and propose a plan.
- Do not make substantial changes to the scientific argument, taxonomy, or manuscript structure without my approval.
- Prefer analyzing and documenting findings before editing existing project files.
- Keep project documentation organized under `docs/`, using appropriate subfolders.
- Avoid duplicating information across documentation files.
- Do not modify original scientific papers or silently overwrite existing research materials.
- When creating or modifying files, clearly report what changed and where.
- Report uncertainties, limitations, and unresolved questions explicitly.
- Save each document in the appropriate `docs/` subfolder according to its purpose.

## Communication
- Be concise and precise.
- Prefer clear, technically accurate language over unnecessarily complex prose.
- Avoid overclaiming and distinguish established findings from interpretations.
- When a decision requires my input, explain the alternatives and their implications before proceeding.
